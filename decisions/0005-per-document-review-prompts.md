# Give each hierarchy document a specialist review prompt and retire the generic reviewer

## Status

Accepted on 2026-09-11.

Supersedes the reviewer-routing portion of [decision 0004](0004-system-design-review-prompt.md); its decision to keep `review-doc-system-design.md` as a dedicated reviewer stands.

## Context

[Decision 0004](0004-system-design-review-prompt.md) reasoned that "the shallowest review sat on the highest-leverage document" and gave the feature system design a dedicated reviewer, while routing product vision, decision record, and learning log to the generic `review-document.md`. That generic pass applies only the terse "Review checklist" bullets for a role; it does not derive a role-specific coverage map, hunt the defect classes that role hides, or exercise the document through the scenarios that catch the most defects. The product vision, Product Requirements Document (PRD), and implementation plan carry consequential, hard-to-reverse decisions — a PRD's requirements and acceptance intent gate every downstream design, and an implementation plan's task graph gates execution — yet each was reviewed by the shallowest pass.

The prompt library is also being pruned: the `record-decision` and `update-learning-log` authoring prompts were removed as unused, so decision records and learning logs no longer have dedicated draft prompts, and the case for a generic reviewer that existed largely to cover them fell away.

## Decision

Add three dedicated, evidence-grounded review prompts — `prompts/review-doc-product-vision.md`, `prompts/review-doc-prd.md`, and `prompts/review-doc-implementation-plan.md` — mirroring the structure and independence discipline of `review-doc-technical-design.md` and `review-doc-system-design.md`. Retire `prompts/review-document.md` so every document in the [documentation hierarchy](../guides/product-documentation-process.md#documentation-hierarchy) is reviewed by a specialist: product vision, PRD, system design, technical design, and implementation plan. Update the prompt catalog, the review-cycle guidance, and the unreleased-changes migration note accordingly.

Each prompt scopes its checks to what the repository's [document contract](../guides/product-documentation-process.md#document-contracts) actually owns. Where the evidence suggested a check the contract does not carry — a vision business-model or go-to-market section, a fixed task-size or day estimate, Gherkin at the PRD level, or a formal-method utility tree — it was deliberately excluded, the same restraint [decision 0004](0004-system-design-review-prompt.md) applied when it declined to import ATAM ritual and [decision 0003](0003-design-longevity-checks.md) applied against speculative generalization.

The review discipline for each prompt is grounded in primary sources and recognized community pillars:

- **PRD review** rests on requirements-engineering standards and inspection evidence. ISO/IEC/IEEE 29148:2018 (https://www.iso.org/standard/72089.html) and IEEE 830-1998 (https://ieeexplore.ieee.org/document/720574) define the requirement quality characteristics the prompt checks — verifiable, unambiguous, complete, consistent, singular, implementation-free, and traceable. Scenario- and perspective-based reading detect materially more requirements defects than ad hoc or checklist reading: Porter, Siy, Mockus and Votta, "Comparing Detection Methods for Software Requirements Inspections," *Empirical Software Engineering* 3 (1998) (https://link.springer.com/article/10.1023/A:1009776104355); Basili et al., "The Empirical Investigation of Perspective-Based Reading," *Empirical Software Engineering* 1 (1996) (https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/BF00368702); Fagan, "Design and Code Inspections," *IBM Systems Journal* 15(3) 1976 (https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1147/sj.153.0182). The outcome-not-solution and testable-story checks draw on the INVEST criteria (https://agilealliance.org/glossary/invest/), Cohn, *User Stories Applied* (2004), and Wiegers, *Software Requirements*. The vision-foreclosure check reuses [decision 0003](0003-design-longevity-checks.md).
- **Product vision review** rests on strategy and product-vision evaluation. Rumelt, *Good Strategy / Bad Strategy* (2011), supplies the diagnosis-and-guiding-policy check and the "fluff" and "mistaking goals for strategy" tells. Cagan, "Vision vs. Strategy," Silicon Valley Product Group (https://www.svpg.com/vision-vs-strategy/), separates vision from strategy and roadmap, grounding the boundary check. Christensen, Hall, Dillon and Duncan, "Know Your Customers' Jobs to Be Done," *Harvard Business Review* (2016) (https://hbr.org/2016/09/know-your-customers-jobs-to-be-done), grounds the users-and-underlying-problems check. Pichler's Product Vision Board (https://www.romanpichler.com/tools/product-vision-board/) informs the concise-and-shared clarity check; Doerr, *Measure What Matters*, informs the falsifiable-success-signal check; Ries, *The Lean Startup* (https://theleanstartup.com/principles), informs treating uncited claims as assumptions rather than facts.
- **Implementation plan review** rests on graph correctness, work-breakdown, and flow evidence. Kahn, "Topological Sorting of Large Networks," *Communications of the ACM* 5(11) 1962 (https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/368996.369025), and Tarjan, "Depth-First Search and Linear Graph Algorithms," *SIAM Journal on Computing* 1(2) 1972, establish the acyclicity and cycle-detection basis. The PMI *Practice Standard for Work Breakdown Structures* 100% rule (https://www.pmi.org/learning/library/practice-standard-work-breakdown-structures-8063) and Critical Path Method (https://www.pmi.org/learning/library/critical-path-method-calculations-scheduling-8040) ground the two-way coverage check; Parnas, "On the Criteria To Be Used in Decomposing Systems into Modules," *CACM* 15(12) 1972 (https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/361598.361623), grounds responsibility mapping. Per-task verifiability draws on INVEST and SMART tasks (https://xp123.com/articles/invest-in-good-stories-and-smart-tasks/) and the inspection evidence above. Small, independent batches and visible dependencies draw on DORA's "Working in Small Batches" and "WIP Limits" capabilities (https://dora.dev/capabilities/working-in-small-batches/, https://dora.dev/devops-capabilities/process/wip-limits/) and DeGrandis, *Making Work Visible* (2017); Cockburn's Elephant Carpaccio (https://alistaircockburn.com/Elephant-Carpaccio) informs vertical, independently deliverable slices.

## Alternatives and costs

Keep `review-document.md` as a fallback for documents outside the hierarchy. Rejected: every reviewed document now has a specialist, the two record types that motivated the fallback lost their authoring prompts in the same pruning, and a role-generic reviewer would duplicate authority the specialists own.

Also add specialist reviewers for decision records and learning logs. Rejected for now: those are records rather than gated convergence documents, and their authoring prompts were removed as unused; a dedicated reviewer would be machinery without a live producer.

Import the source methods wholesale as prompt vocabulary — an ATAM-style quality-attribute utility tree, a full requirements-traceability matrix, PMBOK earned-value scheduling, or Wardley/JTBD frameworks. Rejected: each imports vocabulary the process does not define and would turn a review prompt into a method manual, the same tradeoff [decision 0004](0004-system-design-review-prompt.md) recorded. The prompts capture the underlying checks in plain terms proportional to a single independent reviewer.

## Consequences

Each hierarchy document now receives a review as rigorous as its leverage warrants, with the goal-foreclosure check from [decision 0003](0003-design-longevity-checks.md) carried into the PRD review and reversibility framing kept where it applies. `review-document.md` and its generated bundle are removed; the catalog, review-cycle guidance, and migration notes point each document to its specialist. Decision records and learning logs no longer have dedicated draft or review prompts, matching the pruned library. The empirical base is strongest for requirements and code reviews and for the graph-theoretic plan checks; it is looser for product-vision evaluation, so that prompt favors evidence discipline and defect-class scenario tracing over any single framework's ritual.
